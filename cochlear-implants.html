Cochlear implants

When hearing loss goes beyond what traditional hearing aids can address, an implantable device can open the door to sound again. As a certified Cochlear provider, Dr. Cohen guides patients through every step.

The Basics

What is a cochlear implant?

A cochlear implant is a small, sophisticated electronic device that can restore a sense of sound to someone who is profoundly deaf or severely hard of hearing. Part of it sits behind the ear; a second part is surgically placed under the skin.

It does not restore normal hearing — but it can provide a useful representation of sound that helps you understand speech, recognize warning signals, and reconnect with the world around you.

How the parts work together

  • Microphone — picks up sound from the environment
  • Speech processor — selects and arranges those sounds
  • Transmitter & receiver/stimulator — convert the signals into electric impulses
  • Electrode array — sends the impulses to the auditory nerve

How it differs from a hearing aid

A hearing aid amplifies sound so a damaged ear can detect it. A cochlear implant does something fundamentally different: it bypasses the damaged parts of the ear and stimulates the auditory nerve directly, sending signals to the brain that it learns to recognize as sound.

Hearing through an implant is different from natural hearing and takes time to learn or relearn — but it can dramatically improve awareness of sound and understanding of speech.

Who is a candidate?

Both children and adults who are deaf or severely hard of hearing may be candidates. Adults who lost hearing later in life often do especially well, associating the implant’s signal with sounds they remember — many come to understand speech by listening alone.

For young children, early implantation paired with therapy supports the development of speech, language, and social skills during a critical window.

What to expect

A cochlear implant involves both a surgical procedure and meaningful therapy afterward to learn or relearn hearing. As with any surgery, the risks and costs deserve careful thought, and not all insurance covers the expense.

Results vary from person to person, and audiologists and speech-language pathologists are closely involved in the learning process. Dr. Cohen helps you weigh every factor before implantation and supports you through the rehabilitation that follows.

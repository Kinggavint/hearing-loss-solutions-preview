Dizziness & balance disorders

Feeling unsteady, woozy, or like the room is spinning? Balance is closely tied to the inner ear. Understanding where the problem comes from is the first step to steadier ground.

The Vestibular System

What is a balance disorder?

A balance disorder can make you feel unsteady, giddy, or as though you are moving, spinning, or floating. The source can lie in the brain, the nervous system, or an organ in the inner ear called the labyrinth — a key part of your vestibular (balance) system.

The labyrinth works together with your eyes (the visual system) and your bones and joints (the skeletal system) to keep you oriented. The brain receives, compares, and processes signals from all three to keep you steady — so a problem in any one of them can throw balance off.

Common symptoms

  • Dizziness or vertigo (a spinning sensation)
  • Falling, or the feeling that you might fall
  • Lightheadedness or wooziness
  • Visual blurring and disorientation
  • Nausea, vomiting, or faintness
  • Changes in heart rate, or fear, anxiety, or panic
Common Conditions

Types of balance disorders.

Balance problems can stem from the inner ear (peripheral), the brain and its nerves (central), the body generally (systemic), or blood flow (vascular). A few of the most common:

BPPV

Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo — brief, intense vertigo triggered by a change in head position, such as rolling over in bed or looking up.

Labyrinthitis

An infection or inflammation of the inner ear that causes dizziness and loss of balance.

Ménière’s Disease

An inner-ear fluid disorder causing episodes of vertigo, fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus, and a feeling of fullness in the ear.

Vestibular Neuronitis

An infection of the vestibular nerve, generally viral, that disrupts the balance signals traveling to the brain.

Perilymph Fistula

A leak of inner-ear fluid into the middle ear, which can follow head injury or physical exertion — and sometimes occurs without a known cause.

Dizziness deserves a careful look.

Because balance disorders can have many causes, diagnosis takes care. If you are experiencing dizziness or vertigo, reach out — we will help coordinate the right evaluation and next steps.

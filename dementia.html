Hearing loss, dementia & diabetes

Hearing is deeply connected to the rest of your health. The research linking untreated hearing loss to cognitive decline — and to conditions like diabetes — is one of the most important reasons not to wait.

The Brain Connection

How hearing loss is linked to dementia.

Hearing loss is strongly associated with cognitive decline. Researchers point to three primary ways it can contribute directly to dementia risk — and even a slight, untreated hearing loss can raise that risk substantially.

Even mild untreated hearing loss can increase the risk of developing dementia by as much as 200%.

The encouraging part: today’s leading treatments for restoring hearing may help slow or prevent that decline. Acting early is one of the most protective choices you can make for your brain.

Social isolation

When conversation becomes exhausting, people withdraw. Isolation is itself a major risk factor for cognitive decline.

Cerebral atrophy

When the brain receives less auditory input, the regions that process sound can shrink over time.

Cognitive overload

Straining to decode muffled speech forces the brain to work overtime, leaving fewer resources for memory and thinking.

Warning signs worth acting on

Contact us if you or someone you love notices any of the following:

  • Difficulty hearing when there is background noise
  • The TV or radio needs to be louder for you than for others
  • Trouble following a conversation in the car
  • Frequently asking others to repeat themselves

The diabetes link

Hearing loss is about twice as common in adults with diabetes as in those without. Diabetes can damage the fragile blood vessels of the inner ear, cutting off the blood supply those structures need to function — which can result in hearing loss.

Diabetes-related numbness in the legs can also raise the risk of a balance disorder. If you have diabetes and notice any hearing changes, an evaluation is well worth your time.

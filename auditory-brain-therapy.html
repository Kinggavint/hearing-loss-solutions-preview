Auditory brain therapy

Hearing happens in the brain, not just the ear. Auditory therapy uses specially modified sound to train the brain’s listening skills — and it keeps working between visits.

Brain Plasticity at Work

Training the brain to listen better.

Auditory therapy takes advantage of neuroplasticity — the brain’s ability to reorganize and strengthen with practice — using modified sounds to sharpen how the brain processes what it hears.

It can play a valuable role in the rehabilitation of Auditory Processing Disorder, autism, ADD, and stress and anxiety disorders. In clinical studies, it has also been shown to improve how readily patients accept and succeed with hearing aids.

Who it can help

  • Children and adults with auditory processing disorder
  • Individuals on the autism spectrum
  • Attention difficulties (ADD)
  • Stress and anxiety related to listening effort
  • New hearing aid users adapting to amplified sound
Our Programs

Therapies we’ve researched and chosen.

We have evaluated the field and selected the programs with the strongest track record for auditory improvement.

The Listening Program

Acoustically modified music that systematically exercises the auditory pathways, used to support listening, communication, and self-regulation.

Earobics

Interactive, evidence-based training that builds the phonological and listening skills underlying speech understanding and reading.

LACE (Listening & Communication Enhancement)

Computer-based adult auditory training that sharpens the brain’s ability to follow speech in noise and complements hearing aids rather than replacing them.
